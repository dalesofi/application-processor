# **Profile**

## **Professional identity**

I bring simple solutions to complex problems. I am laser-focused on understanding users, their needs, behaviours and context, and on finding where a product can create the most meaningful impact in their lives and workflows.

I work across the organisation and use data, user insight and different stakeholder perspectives to build a complete picture of the problem. I like working with a team to hypothesise, prototype and test until we find the right solution.

I naturally look for synergies and business opportunities. If I see an opportunity worth pursuing, I will not stay put waiting for someone else to identify it — I will pitch it, investigate it and, when appropriate, lead it.

## **Core strengths**

My strongest capabilities are:

1. Simplifying complex products, workflows and user journeys
2. Cross-functional leadership

Other strong capabilities include:

- UX and customer experience
- Product discovery
- Product strategy
- Product execution
- Experimentation
- Data and analytics
- Stakeholder management
- B2B product
- 0→1 product development
- Scaling products and processes
- AI tools and AI-assisted workflows

## **Product problems I enjoy**

I particularly enjoy taking something messy, ambiguous or inefficient, understanding how all the pieces work, and finding the simplest product solution.

I am especially motivated by problems where an existing journey is not working properly for the user or the business. I enjoy investigating pain points that may initially look like tedious optimisation problems and discovering that, through careful listening and analysis of the product or service design, there is a deeper opportunity.

The most interesting moments are often when fixing one point of friction reveals a much bigger opportunity for the product.

I enjoy:

- Understanding complex systems and journeys
- Finding the root cause behind user or business friction
- Simplifying workflows and experiences
- Connecting user needs with business opportunities
- Turning ambiguity into hypotheses
- Prototyping and testing rather than over-planning
- Finding synergies across teams, products or capabilities
- Improving an existing product when the opportunity is meaningful
- Identifying opportunities proactively and taking initiative to pursue them

## **Problems and environments that drain me**

I am less motivated by:

- Highly regulated domains
- Pure growth optimisation
- Work focused mainly on cutting corners or short-term business optimisation without meaningful product/user value

I can technically work in these environments, but they are less aligned with the type of product work I find motivating.

## **Technical profile**

Technical level: approximately 3.5–4/5.

I am technically fluent but I am not an engineer.

I understand APIs and technical architecture conceptually, can communicate comfortably with engineers, use SQL and Python, and can build small products and prototypes with AI-assisted development tools.

I understand the “grammar” of technology and can potentially pick up new programming languages when needed, but engineering is not where I have spent my career.

My strength is integrating different perspectives — user needs, UX, business, data and technology — and using that understanding to make strong product decisions.

Do not position me as an engineer or imply that software engineering is my primary discipline.

## **AI**

AI is another technology in my product toolkit, not my entire professional identity.

I use AI when it is useful to solve a real problem, accelerate learning, prototype, analyse information, automate repetitive work or build something quickly.

I want to become genuinely stronger over the next 1–2 years in:

- AI product strategy
- Agentic systems
- AI-assisted development
- AI UX

The goal is to become a stronger product leader working with AI, not to become an AI engineer.
