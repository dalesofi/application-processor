# **Preferences**

## **What makes a company attractive**

Prioritise companies with:

- Remote-first or genuinely flexible working
- An interesting product or meaningful problem
- A mission I can understand and get behind
- International teams and environments

I am particularly attracted to companies where I can learn, have meaningful ownership and work with strong people.

## **Application red flags**

### **Industry**

Generally avoid:

- Pharma
- Banking
- War / defence

Also be cautious about highly regulated industries more broadly.

### **Company culture**

Prefer:

- Remote-first
- Low bureaucracy
- High autonomy
- Strong ownership
- Data-informed decision-making
- Direct communication
- Modern product culture

Avoid environments that are overly bureaucratic, highly top-down, culturally outdated or where I have little ability to influence decisions.

### **Role structure**

Ideal:

- Senior-level role
- Meaningful ownership
- Reporting to a VP Product or equivalent senior product leader
- Clear goals and measurable outcomes
- Ability to influence strategy rather than simply execute predefined decisions

Avoid roles that are effectively mid-level despite a senior title.

### **Location**

Preferred:

- Fully remote
- Barcelona-based
- Hybrid in Barcelona with a maximum of approximately 2–3 office days per week

Do not assume willingness to relocate. Relocation is generally not preferred.

### **Compensation**

Minimum serious consideration:

€60,000 gross/year

Target:

€75,000 gross/year

Important: the target should not be treated as a hard requirement if the overall opportunity is unusually strong.

### **Employment**

Preference:

- Permanent employment

Contractor roles can be considered when there is a strong overall match, but permanent roles are preferred.

## **Ideal role**

The ideal role is a Senior Product Manager or equivalent owning a product area end-to-end.

This can also be a clearly defined product area or journey with:

- Clear metrics
- A meaningful user/business problem to solve
- Close proximity to users
- A small, strong team
- Enough autonomy to shape strategy
- Hands-on discovery
- Prototyping and experimentation
- Strong collaboration with design and engineering

The role should involve actual product thinking and decision-making, not simply project coordination or business optimisation.

## **Role types to actively consider**

Actively search across the broader product spectrum, including:

### **Primary**

- Senior Product Manager
- Lead Product Manager
- Product Lead
- Product Manager
- AI Product Manager
- B2B Product Manager
- Consumer Product Manager
- UX / Core UX Product Manager

### **Also consider**

- Product Strategy
- Product Operations
- Product Manager, 0→1
- Program Manager
- Growth Product Manager, when the role is genuinely broader product work rather than pure growth optimisation
- Technical B2B Account Manager / similar commercial roles when the product is genuinely technical and the role involves meaningful product/customer problem-solving

Do not reject a role simply because its title does not match the standard PM taxonomy. Evaluate the actual responsibilities.

## **Roles to deprioritise or avoid**

Avoid roles primarily focused on:

- Pure growth optimisation
- Cutting costs/corners without meaningful product or customer value
- Short-term business optimisation at the expense of product quality or user experience
- Highly regulated domains
- Work where product decisions are entirely top-down
- Roles with little autonomy or ability to influence the product

A role can be technically suitable but still be a poor personal match if the day-to-day work is not meaningful product work.

## **Company stage**

Preferred order:

1. Scale-up
2. Established technology company
3. Early-stage startup / 0→1

However, stage should not be treated as a strict filter.

I can enjoy both:

- Joining an early product and creating structure
- Joining a mature product organisation and improving an existing system

The quality of the problem, team, autonomy and product culture matters more than company stage alone.

## **Ideal working environment**

I perform best with:

- High autonomy
- Small teams
- Remote-first culture
- Little hierarchy
- Strong design culture
- Strong engineering culture
- Data-driven decision-making
- Clear goals
- Direct communication
- Strong ownership
- Ability to challenge assumptions and influence decisions

I become frustrated by:

- Excessive bureaucracy
- Very top-down cultures
- Inability to influence or think independently
- Poor company culture
- Outdated organisational cultures
- Environments where decisions are disconnected from users, data or evidence

## **Stretch-role policy**

Use a balanced approach.

A role can still be recommended when I have strong transferable experience despite 1–2 meaningful domain gaps.

Do not reject a role simply because I have not worked in its exact industry.

However, clearly identify the gaps.

Examples:

- No travel/GDS experience → transferable product/API experience can still justify applying, but travel/GDS should be identified as a gap.
- No production RAG/multi-agent experience → AI/product/technical experience can still justify a stretch application, but production AI architecture should not be implied.
- No marketplace experience → do not call related consumer/growth experience marketplace experience.

Decision categories should be:

- APPLY
- STRETCH
- SKIP

Do not use arbitrary numerical fit scores as the primary decision mechanism.

## **Application integrity rules**

The agent must never:

- Claim I am a Growth PM simply because the job description says “Growth”
- Claim marketplace experience when I do not have it
- Claim production AI or agentic-system experience when I do not have it
- Claim that I owned SaaS
- Claim domain expertise when I do not have it
- Pretend transferable experience is direct experience
- Invent or exaggerate metrics
- Turn contribution into ownership
- Turn a prototype into a production product
- Turn a pilot into a full launch
- Make me sound like an engineer
- Make me sound like a growth specialist
- Use generic corporate language
- Use “passionate” or “excited” without a specific genuine reason
- Hide meaningful gaps when they matter to the application

When I genuinely have domain expertise, highlight it.

The goal is to make the strongest truthful case, not to manufacture a stronger profile.

## **Application positioning**

Applications should be:

- Specific to the actual role
- Evidence-based
- Concise
- Direct
- Human
- Confident without exaggeration

Avoid generic statements such as:

“I am excited to bring my skills and experience to your innovative company.”

Instead, connect a real experience, capability or motivation to a specific problem in the role.

The strongest positioning is usually around:

- Simplifying complexity
- User-centred product thinking
- Cross-functional leadership
- Discovery and experimentation
- Connecting UX, business, data and technology
- B2B product systems
- International/multimarket experience
- Hands-on AI-assisted prototyping and automation

## **Agent optimisation**

Optimise applications using this weighting:

### **60% — Probability of getting an interview**

Prioritise evidence that maps directly to the requirements and demonstrates credible fit.

### **30% — Match to actual strengths**

Prefer roles where the work genuinely aligns with my capabilities, experience and preferred way of working.

### **10% — Personal interest**

Use product, mission, team, learning opportunity and overall attractiveness as a smaller but meaningful factor.

The agent should therefore optimise for **getting interviews for roles that are genuinely plausible and aligned**, rather than maximising either application volume or theoretical job fit.

## **Decision principle**

The agent should answer:

“Is there enough real evidence that Sofia can do this job well, would genuinely want to do it, and can make a credible case for herself?”

If yes → APPLY.

If the core product skills transfer strongly but there are 1–2 meaningful domain gaps → STRETCH.

If the role fundamentally requires experience, motivation or working conditions that conflict with the profile → SKIP.
